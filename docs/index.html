Multi-agent company research that checks its own facts.

Give it a company name and a job posting. Three AI agents research the company, fact-check what they find, and wait for your approval before writing your interview briefing.

How it works

Three agents, and you in the middle

  1. Researcher

    Searches the web for news, funding, tech stack, culture and the role itself, and runs another pass on any category that comes back thin.

  2. Editor

    Checks the key claims with fresh, independent searches and labels every claim. Weak research goes back to the Researcher.

  3. Your review human

    The run pauses. You see every labeled claim and either approve it or send it back with a note.

  4. Writer

    Turns the approved findings into a briefing with sources, and hedges anything that wasn't confirmed.

Before any of this, an optional quick check looks up the company's H-1B sponsorship history in public records. A "yes" has to quote its source word for word.

What makes it different

It would rather say "unverified" than guess

"Verified" has a strict meaning

A claim is marked verified only if its own independent check confirmed every detail it states, not just the gist. That rule is enforced in code, not only asked for in a prompt.

Verified From posting Unverified Contradicted Off-target

Unconfirmed stays unconfirmed

When a check can't confirm something, the claim is labeled unverified and the briefing hedges it instead of stating it as fact. You can see exactly what to double-check before the interview.

Honest about its limits

Verified means a real source states it, not that it is true. A third-party estimate can be verified against the site it came from, so numbers that could be stale still deserve a second look.
